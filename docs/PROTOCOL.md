# TERJ Cross-Spectral Test — Frozen Stage A Protocol

Author: Paweł Majsterek  
Project status: exploratory cross-domain falsification protocol  
Date frozen: 2026-09-29

## 1. Research question

The literal identity

[
	ext{Riemann} = 	ext{Neural Network}
]

is not the hypothesis.

The testable question is whether, after removing differences due to symmetry class,
finite size and arbitrary scale, Riemann-zero spectra and neural-network spectra
retain a common residual relational structure that is not explained by their
respective random-matrix null models.

The working notation is

[
[mathrm{Riemann}]_{mathrm{rel}}
stackrel{?}{simeq}
[mathrm{Neural}]_{mathrm{rel}}.
]

## 2. Null and alternative hierarchy

### H0 — RMT-only explanation

Any similarity between the two systems is fully explained by ordinary
random-matrix universality and finite-size effects.

### H1 — residual local similarity

After standardization against the correct symmetry-specific null, selected local
spectral observables show a common residual pattern.

### H2 — common finite-size scaling

Residual observables admit a shared scaling law across Riemann height/effective
dimension and neural width.

### H3 — common effective spectral dynamics

After time reparameterization and null subtraction, heat-flow motion of Riemann
zeros and training-time motion of neural eigenvalues admit the same effective
dynamical law.

### H4 — relational universality beyond the null

A multiscale residual feature distribution is statistically closer between
Riemann and Neural than either is to matched synthetic controls, and the
relationship survives out-of-sample prediction.

Stage A tests only the static part of H0/H1. It does not test H2-H4.

## 3. Mandatory symmetry separation

Riemann zeros are compared with a unitary beta=2 reference.

Real neural Gram matrices

[
G=W_2^	op W_2
]

are compared with a real Wishart/Laguerre beta=1 reference.

A raw match between uncorrected spectra is not accepted as evidence.

For every scalar feature (S_j), define a null-standardized residual

[
z_j = rac{S_j-mu_{0,j}}{sigma_{0,j}},
]

where the null mean and standard deviation are estimated from the matched
ensemble at the same finite size.

The object compared across domains is the vector

[
z=(z_1,ldots,z_p),
]

not the raw spectrum.

## 4. Stage A primary endpoint

The primary endpoint is the distance between group-level residual centroids

[
D_{RN}=|ar z_R-ar z_N|_2.
]

This value is calibrated against a Monte Carlo null generated by drawing
synthetic residual groups from the matched null controls.

The primary Stage A result is therefore a calibrated tail probability for
whether the observed cross-domain distance is unusually small or unusually
large relative to the control construction.

A small distance alone is not sufficient for a TERJ interpretation. It must
also be shown that both empirical groups differ from their synthetic controls
in a coherent direction.

## 5. Secondary endpoints

Secondary diagnostics are:

- energy distance,
- Gaussian-kernel Maximum Mean Discrepancy (MMD),
- leave-one-group-out nearest-centroid identification,
- feature-wise residual correlations,
- cross-prediction error,
- sensitivity to removal of one feature at a time.

Secondary tests are exploratory unless a later protocol revision explicitly
promotes them before results are viewed.

## 6. Feature policy

Stage A uses only observables present in both domains under the same mathematical
definition.

Features may include:

- mean consecutive gap ratio,
- median consecutive gap ratio,
- small-gap fractions,
- spacing variance after a declared normalization,
- pair-correlation ordinates at predeclared bins,
- number variance at predeclared L,
- spectral rigidity at predeclared L.

A feature must not be inserted merely because it happens to look similar after
results are inspected.

If a feature is unavailable in either domain, it is omitted from the primary
vector rather than replaced by a non-equivalent proxy.

## 7. Replication units

For neural data, the network initialization seed is the inferential unit.

For Riemann data, independent or non-overlapping zero blocks are the intended
replication units.

Individual eigenvalue gaps or zero spacings are not treated as independent
cross-domain replicates.

## 8. Blind identification

A later Stage A extension will construct anonymized feature vectors from:

- Riemann,
- Neural,
- GUE,
- GOE,
- real Wishart,
- Poisson.

Labels are withheld from the classifier during feature construction.

The scientifically interesting outcome is not merely inability to distinguish
Riemann from GUE or Neural from Wishart. The target is residual similarity after
the symmetry-specific null has been removed.

## 9. Stopping rule

Stage A does not justify Stage B/C merely because plots appear similar.

Progression to finite-size cross-scaling requires all of the following:

1. residual-vector construction is valid in both domains;
2. no single feature dominates the result;
3. the result survives leave-one-feature-out analysis;
4. at least one out-of-sample prediction is successful;
5. the observed similarity is not reproduced by matched null controls.

If these conditions fail, the result is reported as negative or unresolved.

## 10. Interpretation boundary

Even a positive Stage A result would not establish that Riemann zeros and neural
networks are the same system, share a physical substrate, or prove TERJ.

A positive result would support only the narrower statement that two different
systems exhibit a shared residual statistical geometry under the tested feature
map and null calibration.

This distinction is part of the protocol and must be retained in publication.
